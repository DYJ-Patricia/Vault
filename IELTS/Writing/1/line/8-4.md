The line chart illustrates the changes in the volume of commodities, quantified in millions of tonnes, that were transported in the UK by four modes of delivery, including road, water, rail, and pipeline, between 1974 and 2002.

Overall, the volumes of goods transported by all four methods experienced significant increases, except for rail, which returned to its initial level by the end of the period. Notably, road and pipeline transport led and trailed in terms of volume throughout this timeframe, while water transport surpassed rail after being equal in the first four years.

In 1974, approximately 70 million tonnes of commodities were transported via road, compared to just 5 million tonnes by pipeline, representing a 14-fold difference. Road shipping initially rose but fell back to its starting value around 1980, while pipeline transportation saw a dramatic increase, reaching about 15 million tonnes. Subsequently, pipeline transport experienced two extended periods of plateauing between 1980 and 1992, and again from 1995 to 2002, with a brief rise in between. In contrast, road shipping exhibited a more or less continuous surge, reaching nearly 100 million tonnes by 2002.

In the first four years, the volume of goods moved by water and rail remained relatively stable, both hovering around 40 million tonnes until 1978. After this point, the trends diverged: water transport increased to nearly 57 million tonnes by 1982, while rail dropped to 30 million tonnes. In the following years, water transport plateaued for nearly 16 years before finally surging to approximately 62 million tonnes. Meanwhile, rail shipping hit its lowest point in 1994, at 30 million tonnes, before recovering to its initial value of 40 million tonnes by 2002.
